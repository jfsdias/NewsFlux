# Benchmark readiness framework V1 — proposed research governance

The historical 12-positive / three-component / 0.50-share heuristic remains historical evidence and is superseded for future readiness. Discovery V2 (at least one SAME_EVENT) is descriptive existence only. Neither is a future readiness gate. All 51 existing reviews and D2G0 observations are hypothesis-generation evidence, not independent validation.

BRF V1 asks whether a preregistered rule's one-sided upper 95% confidence bound for unsafe or unconfirmed predicted merges is at most 5%. SAME_EVENT is confirmed safe; RELATED_TOPIC, DIFFERENT_EVENT and UNCERTAIN all count against confirmed safety. Alpha and the research bound are each 0.05. These are research governance criteria, not a production false-merge threshold. No production threshold or fixed design-effect multiplier is set. False-merge cost exceeds false-split cost.

The primary estimand is the fraction of predicted-merge edges in the frozen eligible future window that are unsafe or unconfirmed after adjudication. Partition the complete candidate graph into connected components using only the frozen pre-label candidate construction. The primary sampling unit is the component; all predicted merge edges within each sampled component receive independent duplicate human review. Restrict the sampling frame to the complete frozen list of components with at least one predicted merge; report the count of zero-merge components separately. Randomly sample components without replacement from that frame, with recorded, equal nonzero inclusion probabilities. If the entire population is manageable, review every predicted merge instead. If the resulting population or a component exceeds the approved safe review budget, stop for PM; do not silently omit or thin edges.

For a sampled component mark it unsafe when any predicted merge within it is unsafe or unconfirmed. At the single preregistered final look, invert the exact hypergeometric distribution to obtain a one-sided 95% upper bound K on the number of unsafe components in the finite frozen component population. A conservative edge-risk bound is the sum of the K largest component predicted-merge sizes divided by the total number of predicted-merge edges, capped at one. This worst-case conversion does not assume that unsafe components have typical size and does not equate edge count with independent units. The exact inversion, treatment of zero-merge components, integer rounding, admissible population, algorithm, tests and deterministic frame-to-sample-size rule must be frozen and independently verified before any future validation capture. The realized sample size is derived only from the completed label-blind frame and frozen before labels. This bounds risk in the frozen window, not an unqualified future-time population.

If the frozen window contains zero predicted merges, the primary merge-risk estimand is undefined and the result is INCONCLUSIVE; no zero-denominator PASS is permitted. A component census yields exact observed finite-window risk. Report cluster count, edge count and actual sampled units separately. Report the independent Bernoulli references 0/59 and approximately 1/93 only for genuinely independent Bernoulli units; neither is automatically a pair-count target. Consider component collapsing and block/cluster bootstrap as sensitivity analyses, never a substitute for the primary conservative finite-population bound. No arbitrary cluster multiplier is allowed.

The single final look prevents optional-stopping inflation: preregister the deterministic sample-size rule and maximum review budgets before capture; freeze the resulting sample size and inclusion probabilities after capture and before labeling, with no interim outcome-based stopping. If complete sample units cannot be reviewed, sampling probabilities are unknown, the exact inversion has not been independently verified, or the resulting upper bound remains above 5% without decisive evidence of excess, return INCONCLUSIVE. Do not use a weaker interval or infer unseen labels. Independent statistical review and PM approval are required before the future validation contract is considered executable.

A proposed mandatory cluster guard is FALSE_FAMILY_MERGE_COUNT=0 in the reviewed components, where a false family merge is an adjudicated component that joins distinct concrete events through predicted merge edges. This sample observation cannot certify unseen components; the conservative risk bound addresses the latter. PM must approve the precise adjudication procedure and guard before capture.

## Label-blind freeze barriers

**PRE_CAPTURE_FREEZE:** Before the validation window, commit the capture span and pair window; complete candidate rule and extractor versions; graph construction and eligible-component definition; unsafe/unconfirmed outcome; SRSWOR algorithm and seed derivation policy; exact hypergeometric inversion and component-to-edge conversion; BRF alpha and delta; maximum component and total-edge review budgets; oversized-component STOP rule; deterministic realized-frame-to-sample-size rule; reviewer and adjudication protocols; analysis code and coverage tests. No outcome labels exist.

**POST_CAPTURE_PRE_LABEL_FRAME_FREEZE:** After the complete window is captured and the frozen rule is applied, but before any human label, freeze the article-frame, candidate-edge-frame and component-frame hashes; component count N, predicted-edge count and each component's edge weight; census-versus-sample decision; realized sample size m from the preregistered rule; inclusion probabilities; seed instantiated under the frozen policy; exact sampled component IDs and review-edge set. The realized m and inclusion probabilities are not known before capture. References to freezing seeds before the window mean the derivation policy; the instantiated seed is frozen at this post-capture, pre-label barrier. No parameter can be changed in response to labels.

If the realized frame violates a preregistered storage, component-size, sample-size or edge-review budget: **STOP_TO_PM**. Do not thin articles, drop large components, change candidate thresholds, opportunistically reduce m, change sampling probabilities or start labeling.
